# Zero-SWARM TCP Sequence/Acknowledgment UInt32 — 2026-08-27

## Outcome

Accepted `zenodo_zeroswarm_tcp_sequence_u32`: native TCP sequence and
acknowledgment trajectories from the two normal Factory I/O Modbus/TCP
captures in Zenodo record 15082260.

The recipe emits eight natural samples containing 6,071,882 unsigned 32-bit
values and 24,287,528 primary bytes. Sequence numbers and acknowledgments are
separate series; each capture is split into request and response directions.

## New numeric shape

This family adds cumulative TCP byte-position staircases. A stream contains a
large connection-specific initial offset followed by long plateaus and small,
payload-sized advances. The dominant nonzero steps are 11, 12, 13, 15, and 17
bytes. Across both captures every retained stream is monotonic, while plateau
counts range from one to 473,453.

This behavior is distinct from the existing uint16 Zero-SWARM register values,
packet lengths, receive windows, IPv4 identifiers, and Modbus transaction
fields. It is also unlike generic IDs or timestamps: TCP sequence space is a
transport-state counter whose increments directly reflect acknowledged byte
progress and packet exchange structure.

## Source, rights, and privacy

Both exact PCAPs and the record metadata are pinned by size, MD5, and SHA-256.
The Zenodo record declares CC BY 4.0. The captures come from a synthetic
Factory I/O testbed, not real user communications.

The parser uses addresses and port 502 only to identify packet direction; it
does not emit IP/MAC addresses, ports, timestamps, checksums, TCP flags or
options, packet bodies, or Modbus values.

## Representation and verification

TCP sequence and acknowledgment fields are protocol-native network-order
uint32 values. Each is serialized unchanged in canonical little-endian order;
no relative-sequence normalization, delta transform, rebasing, or packet
concatenation is applied.

Verification reparses all 3,035,941 qualifying packets across the two files
and pins source identities, packet counts, stream boundaries, counts, ranges,
cardinalities, transition and decrease counts, per-sample hashes, aggregate
hashes, index rows, filenames, and every generated byte.
